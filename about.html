The studio · JP Nagar, Bangalore

The photographer,
and the studio he built

AKV Photography was founded in Bangalore in 2012 by Anil Kumar Venkatesh. Fourteen years later it is still the same idea it started with: photograph the wedding that is actually happening, not the one a shot list says should be.

Crafting stories through visuals. Candid photography, traditional coverage, cinematic films, pre-wedding and maternity shoots, and albums built to be opened again in twenty years.

How it started

One photographer,
and a stubborn idea

Anil Kumar Venkatesh started the studio in 2012 on a belief that sounds obvious and almost never survives a wedding day: the best photographs are of things that were already happening.

Fourteen years and several hundred functions later, that is still how AKV works. What that looks like on the day: the uncle laughing at something off-frame. The bride’s mother checking the pleats one last time. The two-second gap between the ritual ending and everyone realising it has ended.

Alongside it runs the traditional work, because a wedding album that only contains candid frames is a wedding album that is missing the photographs the family will actually ask for. Both get done, properly, by people whose job that day is only that.

The team has grown into photographers and cinematographers who will travel to your destination to cover every function, and their knowledge of the latest equipment and techniques is what lets them preserve the day without ever standing in the middle of it.

The studio works out of JP Nagar 8th Phase and travels outstation for destination weddings. It shoots the muhurtham, the mehendi, the haldi, the engagement and the reception with the same attention, and hands the whole story back within four weeks.

How the studio works

Seven things we do
the same way every time

None of it is complicated. Most of it is just refusing to cut the corner that everybody else cuts.

  • We talk before we shoot. Dates, venues, families, the rituals that matter and the ones you would rather we photograph from a distance.
  • We never stage a moment twice. If it happened, we caught it or we did not. We do not ask a room to redo it.
  • We never block a ritual. The ceremony belongs to the family, not to the camera.
  • Candid and traditional get separate eyes. One person cannot do both well at the same instant, so we do not pretend otherwise.
  • Every function gets full attention. The mehendi is not a warm-up for the wedding.
  • Four weeks, and you do not chase us. Colour-graded photographs, films and albums, from the last event day.
  • The album is designed, not dumped. Hand-sequenced spreads, so the story reads the way the day actually felt.
