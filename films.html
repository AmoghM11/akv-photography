How a film gets made

Shot quietly,
cut carefully

A wedding film is not a second camera following the photographer around. It is its own crew, its own plan, and its own edit.

Before the day we agree what the film has to carry — which rituals are non-negotiable, which family members must be in it, whether the grandparents need the full uncut muhurtham or the four-minute version. That conversation decides where the cameras stand.

On the day the film team moves the same way the photography team does: no blocking a ritual, no restaging a moment that already happened, no asking a room to do something twice for the camera.

  • 4K throughout — shot, cut and delivered at full resolution.
  • Teaser first — a short cut you can send out while the wedding is still being talked about.
  • Highlight film — the day at its natural length, scored and graded.
  • Traditional full-length — the ceremony uncut, for the family who want every minute.
  • Drone and aerials — cleared with the venue beforehand, on request.
  • Live streaming — the ceremony in real time for whoever could not travel.
  • Four-week delivery — the same standard as the photographs.
